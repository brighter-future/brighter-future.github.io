Taking notes that help you remember

Good notes are short, in your own words, and easy to find again. You do not need to write everything the teacher says.

Before the lesson

  • Write the date and the topic at the top of the page.
  • Look at last lesson's notes for two minutes so the new ideas have somewhere to connect.

During the lesson

  • Listen first, then write. Wait for the end of an idea and write it in one line.
  • Use your own short forms: b/c for because, an arrow for "leads to".
  • Copy every worked example the teacher puts on the board. Examples are what you will practise from.
  • Put a question mark in the margin next to anything you do not understand, and ask before the day ends.

After the lesson

  1. The same day, read your notes once and underline the three most important lines.
  2. Cover the page and try to say those three lines out loud.
  3. Explain the lesson to a friend or family member. If you can teach it, you know it.
Try this: split your page with a line down the left side. Notes go on the right. After class, write one question on the left for each idea. Later, cover the right side and answer your own questions.
