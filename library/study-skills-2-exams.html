Preparing for an exam

Studying a little on many days works far better than studying a lot on one night. Your brain needs sleep between sessions to store what you learned.

Two weeks before

  • List every topic that could be in the exam. Tick the ones you already know well.
  • Plan short sessions of 25 minutes with a 5 minute break. Start with the topics you did not tick.

How to study

  1. Test yourself. Close the book and write down everything you remember, then check. This works better than reading the page again.
  2. Do past questions. Time yourself. Mark your own answers honestly.
  3. Mix topics. Do a few fractions questions, then some equations, then back to fractions.
  4. Study with a friend. Take turns asking each other questions.

The night before

  • Pack your pens, pencil, ruler and calculator.
  • Read your underlined notes once, then stop. Go to sleep early.

In the exam

  • Read the whole paper first. Start with a question you find easy.
  • Show your working. Marks are often given for the method even when the final answer is wrong.
  • If you are stuck, move on and come back. Leave no question blank.
